02 / Ladner's theorem

There is a middle ground.

NP-complete problems are at least as hard as every problem in NP.

The theorem

What it says

If P ≠ NP, then some problem in NP is neither in P nor NP-complete.

Such a problem is called NP-intermediate, or NPI. It is not solvable in polynomial time, but it is not as hard as every problem in NP.

The proof constructs a special problem to show that NPI problems must exist. It does not tell us whether any familiar problem is NPI.

03 / Possible examples

Natural candidates.

These problems are in NP and are often discussed as possible NPI problems.

Graph isomorphism

Are two graphs the same after renaming their vertices? A proposed renaming can be checked quickly. We do not know whether the problem is in P or NP-complete.

NPI candidate

Integer factorization

For example, ask whether an integer N has a divisor between 2 and k. A proposed divisor is easy to check. We do not know whether this decision problem is in P or NP-complete.

NPI candidate

Important: Neither problem is known to be NPI. Ladner's theorem guarantees that some NPI problem exists if P ≠ NP, but these candidates may or may not be examples.
