Is NP split cleanly in two?

If $\P \ne \NP$, then NP is not split cleanly into “easy” problems (P) and “hardest” problems (NP-complete): there is a nonempty middle ground, NPI. This site proves that fact (Ladner’s Theorem) and then proves our centerpiece, Mahaney’s Theorem: unless $\P = \NP$, no sparse set can be NP-hard. Together they show that the structure of NP is richer than a two-way split, and that density is a real barrier to hardness.
